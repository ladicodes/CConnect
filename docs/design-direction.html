Connect-Ask · Phase B · Design direction for review

Many colours, one point of focus.

The Covenant Connect logo is six rays converging on a single word. Connect-Ask works the same way: many people, many kinds of participation, all meeting at one clear place. The design uses that idea structurally, through colour roles and composition, rather than by repeating the rays as decoration.

This is a static review board, not the app. The mockups use the real logo.png. For preview, the empty margin is trimmed in the browser and the off-white background is hidden with a CSS blend. The app will use a properly transparent file, pending your approval.

1 Direction

Warm, editorial, youthful but composed. It should feel like a well-made church bulletin that became an app: confident type, generous space, quiet surfaces, and one brand colour speaking at a time.

One ray, one meaning

Each participation type owns one logo colour. The colour appears as a small ray tick (a single logo ray pointing at its label) and in that section's states. Only the homepage shows several at once, echoing the logo.

Navy does the work

Navy is the only brand colour dark enough for text and buttons (9.7:1). It carries every primary action, link and focus ring. The bright rays never carry text, which keeps everything readable.

Lists, not card piles

Questions and testimonies are typographic lists separated by hairlines. Surfaces stay flat, with 8–12px corners and almost no shadows. Hierarchy comes from type size, weight and spacing.

2 Colour

Sampled directly from logo.png. Each bright ray gets a darker text variant that passes WCAG AA (≥4.5:1) and a pale tint for backgrounds. Ratios are measured against the paper colour.

3 Typography

Two families, both self-hosted through next/font, so there are no runtime requests to Google and no layout shift on slow church Wi-Fi.

Display · Bricolage Grotesque

Ask what you’ve been wondering.

It has character, and its slightly quirky grotesque forms feel young and human without being childish. Optical sizing keeps it crisp from phone headings to projector scale. It sits well beside the logo’s geometric wordmark without imitating it.

Text & UI · Figtree

If God already knows what I’m going to pray, why should I still pray? Figtree is friendly and geometric, with open letterforms that stay legible at small sizes on cheaper Android screens and for older members.

TestimoniesEditorial pull-quotes in the display face, with no likes or counts. Only items with the member’s consent and a leader’s approval appear. The display name is optional.
Mobile navigation

A bottom tab bar with four labelled destinations: Home · Questions · Prayer · Testimonies. It sits in thumb reach, and every label is visible, never icon-only. Focused forms hide the bar to make room for the pinned submit button. On desktop the same four items become a top bar, with an “Ask a question” button on the right.

Add Context

It isn’t a tab, so the bar stays at four items. You reach it from the homepage list and from an “Add context to this question” button on each question’s page, which links your note to that exact question.

Prayer

It uses the same form pattern as Ask, with a green tick and a privacy statement above the field: “Only church leaders can read this. It is never shown publicly.” There are no name, email or phone fields.

5 Admin: moderation workspace

The admin side is built for speed during a live service. It has an inbox-style split view, keyboard shortcuts, and filters kept in the URL so auto-refresh never resets them. Board visibility and Answer status are two separate controls, because they are separate concepts.

The Overview page uses the same shell. It shows a row of “needs attention” counts, a live-service panel with Activate/Close, QR and Presenter shortcuts, and a short recent-activity log. There are no charts. On mobile the split view collapses to list → detail, and the sidebar becomes a menu sheet.

6 Projector screens

Light backgrounds on purpose. The official logo has a dark wordmark that would vanish on a dark screen, and we won’t alter it. Light slides also project well in lit auditoriums. If the church has a reversed (white) logo, a dark LED theme can be added later.
